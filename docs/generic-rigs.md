# Generic Rigs

Characters don't have to be humanoids. Any rigged model that isn't built and animated as a person (on four legs, two, or more) can be prepared, on a Generic rig (Unity's word for any skeleton that isn't its Humanoid), as a character from its model and its own animations: see [A generic rig of your own](#a-generic-rig-of-your-own). The first is a fox: a wolf-sized, low-poly fox with flat-coloured regions (an orange coat, a white chest, muzzle and tail tip, dark legs, ear tips and nose), on four legs, with its own body, movement and animations, prepared the same way.

This page is in three parts. The first, [A generic rig of your own](#a-generic-rig-of-your-own), is how a generic-rig character is prepared. The second is the fox as a character and its paw placement. Both are the package's. The third, [Generating the fox (the model pipeline)](#generating-the-fox-the-model-pipeline), is how the fox's model is made: generated in Blender by scripts, as the mannequin is. That isn't part of the package.

## A generic rig of your own

The steps are in [Adding a Character](adding-a-character.md#a-generic-rig-not-a-humanoid): put the model in a folder of its own in `Models/Generic`, in [the output folder](getting-started.md#the-output-folder), check the setup Prepare Generic Rig Models makes for it, then make it a character with New Character. This section is what each part does.

**Preparing.** A model file with a skinned mesh in a folder under the output folder's `Models/Generic` is prepared by itself when it's added or changed, or when its setup or animation set changes (`GenericRigModelPostprocessor`); **Tools > Meridian Motion > Prepare > Generic Rig Models** prepares them all (or `-executeMethod MeridianMotion.EditorTools.GenericRigModelImporter.PrepareAllFromCommandLine`, with `-model <path>` for one). Its first import is as a Generic rig, each of its takes a looping clip named after the take; after that its import settings are its own, so a clip set not to loop in its Animation tab stays so. From its setup, Prepare Generic Rig Models (`GenericRigModelImporter`) makes, beside the model and named after its file:
- **its model prefab** (`[name] Model.prefab`), made anew each time: the model with its Animator playing its controller (no root motion: the movement moves it), an `Eyes` point on its eyes bone (which the character's eye point and the camera follow: `CharacterModelSlot.EyesName`), its paw placement (LegPlacementIK, see [Paw placement](#paw-placement)) and its hitboxes (see [Hitboxes](animation.md#hitboxes));
- **its Animator Controller**, built from its animation set (or its layout: see [A generic rig's animations](#a-generic-rigs-animations));
- **its translator** (`[name] Animator Translator.asset`), made only if it's missing, then yours to change: the humanoid's parameters, and its turn speed (what its standing turn blends by);
- **its body** (`[name] Body.asset`), fitted from its mesh only if it's missing, then yours to tune (see [Its body](#its-body)); delete it to fit it again;
- **its model source** (`[name].asset`), which a character wears: kept at the model's size, standing on its feet at its origin.

Each generic-rig character is prepared again only when its model, setup, animation set or layout has changed since it was last prepared (on this machine). The Console has a line from `[Prepare Generic Rig Models]` for each: what it made, and what it couldn't find.

### Its setup

A **Generic Rig Setup** (`[name] Setup.asset`, beside the model; Create > Meridian Motion > Models > Generic Rig Setup for one by hand) says what the generic-rig character is made of. Each bone is named as it is in the model (as the Hierarchy shows it):

| Setting | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Model | The model file it prepares. |
| Root Bone | The bone everything else hangs from (the hips or pelvis): its paw placement pitches and rolls the body about it. |
| Legs | Each leg: its Upper bone (thigh or upper arm), Lower bone (shin or forearm) and Foot (the paw, or a hind leg's foot below the hock); its Bend Direction (the way its middle joint points, in the model's space: back for a front leg's elbow, forward for a hind leg's stifle); its Contact (where its paw touches the ground, in the model's space as it stands: its toes, for a paw that rolls over them); and its Wall Clearance (how far its paw's mesh reaches beyond its contact toward a wall it runs along). |
| Eyes Bone, Eyes Position | The bone its eyes are on (its head), and where they are, in the model's space as it stands: the eye point the camera follows, and its prefab's eye height while its model loads. |
| Counter Pitch Bones | Bones turned back against the body's pitch on slopes (a tail, a neck), so they don't dig into the slope or point at the sky: by a share nose up and a share nose down, and a tail's tip kept above the ground. |
| Turn Bend Bones | Bones bent into a turn, each by its share: the spine, neck and head toward it, the tail away, so it curves into the turn. |
| Hitbox Bones | The bones its hitboxes go on. Empty: every bone carrying enough of its mesh. |
| Animation Set | Its clips by slot: see [A generic rig's animations](#a-generic-rigs-animations). |
| Layout | Or an Animator Layout of its own, whose controller it plays, when it has no animation set (the fox's is `Fox Layout.asset`). |
| Unfound | What preparing couldn't find (bones it names that the model hasn't, a leg missing a bone, no idle): written each time it's prepared, and shown as a warning at the top of its inspector. |

Its inspector also has **Prepare It Now**, **Guess Again** (its bones and clips guessed afresh, replacing what's set; its animation set kept) and **Open Its Animation Set**.

**Guessing.** A setup made by Prepare Generic Rig Models starts from guesses, from the names most rigs use:
- its feet are the bones named for a foot, paw, hoof or hand (not a toe or an end bone), the bone above each its lower bone and the one above that its upper;
- a leg is a front one if its names say so (front, fore, arm, hand) and a hind one if they say hind, back, rear or thigh, else if its foot is ahead of the hips; it's a left one if its names say so (`_L`, `.L`, `Left`, `L_`, ...), else if it's left of the middle; the legs are listed front then hind, left then right;
- each leg bends the way it's bent as the model stands (or, standing straight, as a front leg's elbow or a hind leg's stifle does), and its paw touches the ground under its furthest-forward toe;
- its root bone is the one named hips or pelvis (else where its legs meet), its eyes bone the one named head (its eyes in the middle of its head's mesh, halfway to its front), its neck and first tail bone are turned against the pitch, and its spine, neck, head and first tail bones bent into turns;
- its animation set's roles are filled with the clips (in its model file, or any other file in its folder) whose names say them, each clip once: see below.

Most rigs need only a check. Anything it couldn't find is listed in Unfound.

### A generic rig's animations

A generic-rig character plays its own clips: the humanoid's are made for a person's skeleton. Its **animation set** (made by Prepare Generic Rig Models, `[name] Animation Set.asset`: see [Animation sets](animation.md#animation-sets)) starts from the generic rig layout, `Assets/MeridianMotion/Content/Animation/Generic Rig Layout.asset`: the fox's states and transitions (standing and turning, walking to sprinting by speed, crouching, jumping and falling, swimming, wall-running), with empty clip slots. Its slots, and the words in a clip's name that guess them: Idle (idle, stand, rest), Walk (walk), Run (run, trot, jog, canter), Sprint (gallop, sprint, dash), Turn Step (turn: stepping round on the spot), Crouch Idle and Crouch Walk (crouch, sneak, stalk, crawl), Jump Rise (jump, rise) and Jump Fall (fall), Swim Idle (swim idle, tread, float) and Swim (swim, paddle). Drop a clip on a slot in the set's inspector to change it.

Only Idle is needed: a role left empty plays its fallback's clip (Run the Walk's, Sprint the Run's, the crouches, jumps and swims the Idle's or Walk's: each slot's Fallback Slot in the layout), and with no Turn Step it stands still as it turns. The layout is Only For Sets: it's never built by itself (Build Animator Controllers skips it), only into the sets that start from it.

Its walk, run, sprint and swim are blended by speed at the fox's speeds (the layout's Locomotion and Swim thresholds). If your generic-rig character's clips are made for other speeds (its paws slide over the ground), copy the layout, set its thresholds to the speeds its clips are made for, and point its set's Layout at the copy. Its movement's speeds (Walking & Running's and Swimming's, on its movement mode) are tuned to match, as the fox's are.

### Its body

What Prepare Generic Rig Models fits (`BodyFitting.GenericRig`), when the generic-rig character has no body yet, is rounded, with no corners to catch on things, and of several parts, as the fox's is (see [What the fox is](#what-the-fox-is)): capsules lying along its back, as long as its torso and as round as it's wide, stacked to fill its depth (side by side, for one wider than it's deep); and a sphere for its head, as round as its head is wide, resting on its underside (its ears aren't the body's). Its torso is the mesh its spine carries, with its haunches and shoulders (the mesh its legs' upper bones carry above their top joints); its head, the mesh its eyes bone carries. The capsules reach down to a step above its feet (the Character prefab's Max Step Height), whatever its belly's height: anything taller than a step meets the body, and its legs, under it, are held up over steps (Hover Over Steps). Tune it in its Body Definition: it isn't fitted again while it's there.

### Its character

New Character makes it a character (see [Adding a Character](adding-a-character.md#a-generic-rig-not-a-humanoid)): a variant of Character.prefab wearing its model source, whose MMotionMotor has its body (the model source's Body Definition) and Hover Over Steps on, and whose CharacterModelSlot has Body Fit off (its body as defined, not fitted to the model) and its eye height (from its setup) while its model loads (`NewCharacter.SaveVariant`). Its movement starts as a copy of the fox's (Copy The Fox's): the humanoid's with what a fox can't do turned off. A generic-rig character that can do more turns those abilities back on in its copy, and the IK an ability needs on a humanoid (hands on a ledge, say) isn't a generic-rig character's: see [Paw placement](#paw-placement) for the IK a generic-rig character has, and [IK for your own model](animation.md#ik-for-your-own-model) for IK components to copy and build on.

## Playing the fox

`Assets/Showcase/Prefabs/Fox.prefab` is a character like `Character.prefab` (it's a variant of it), with the fox's body, movement mode and model. Its body is its MMotionMotor's Body Definition, and its CharacterModelSlot's Body Fit is off, so the fox keeps that body rather than taking one from its model (see [Replacing the model](animation.md#replacing-the-model)). To play it in a scene, set the scene's character spawner's Character Prefab to it (see [Spawning the character](spawning.md)); the camera follows it as it does the humanoid, from its eyes.

## What the fox is

- **Its body** (`Assets/Showcase/Models/Generic/Fox/Fox Body.asset`, a Body Definition, set on Fox.prefab's MMotionMotor; tuned by hand in the asset, which the fox's generator makes only if it's missing) is rounded, with no corners to catch on things: its back is two capsules lying along it, one above the other (it's narrower than it's deep), from its rump to its chest, and its head a sphere ahead of them, as high as its head is (not its ears or its tail): a shape of several parts (see [Collision](movement.md#collision)). It's held above its legs: the lower capsule's bottom is a step above its paws, which only hovering over steps stands on (so the fox keeps Hover Over Steps on), its paws kept on the ground by probes. It's a shape the movement moves, not a collider: what meets the fox meets its model's hitboxes, on its body, head, tail and legs (see [Hitboxes](animation.md#hitboxes)). Up and over a ramp and up a flight of stairs its paws stay on the ground; down a flight as steep as the levels', its back end, well behind its paws, rests on the steps behind, its paws a little above the treads under them (the model's paw placement reaches them down). Being longer than it's wide, it turns only as far as it has room to (see [Collision](movement.md#collision)).
- **Its movement** is `Assets/Showcase/Movement/Fox Movement.asset`: the humanoid's movement mode with what a fox can't do turned off (ledges, vaulting, climbing, ladders, pushing and pulling, ropes, ziplines, wall slides, rolling and dashing) and its own tuning: it trots at its Walk Speed and gallops sprinting (Run Speed), jumps once, crouches (stalks) without sliding, swims and wall-runs (see below). Tune it there.
- **Its model** is `Assets/Showcase/Models/Generic/Fox/Fox.fbx`, prepared from its setup (`Fox Setup.asset`) as any generic-rig character is, a Generic rig (not a Unity Humanoid): Hips, Spine1 and Spine2, Neck, Head, Muzzle, the ears, four tail bones, and each leg's bones (FrontUpperLeg, FrontLowerLeg and FrontPaw; HindUpperLeg, HindLowerLeg, HindFoot and HindPaw; each `_L` and `_R`). Its model prefab carries a transform named Eyes on its head, which the character's eye point follows (a model that isn't a humanoid marks its eyes so: see `CharacterModelSlot.EyesName`).
- **Its animations** are in place and loop: Idle, Walk, Trot, Gallop, Jump Rise, Jump Fall, Crouch Idle, Crouch Walk, Turn Step (stepping in place, for turning on the spot), Swim and Swim Idle (a dog paddle, and treading water). Walking, trotting and stalking, its tail sways gently side to side once a stride, rippling down it, counterbalancing its legs; galloping, it runs flat out, its neck lowered and stretched forward, its ears folded back, its tail streaming out behind. Its Animator Controller (`Fox.controller`) plays Locomotion (Idle, or Turn Step as it turns standing, by TurnSpeed; then Walk, Trot and Gallop by Speed), Crouch (Crouch Idle and Crouch Walk by Speed, while Crouching), Swim (Swim Idle and Swim by Speed, while Swimming), Wall Run (Locomotion's gaits by Speed, while WallRunning) and Airborne (Jump Fall and Jump Rise by VerticalSpeed, while not Grounded, Swimming or WallRunning), driven by its own Animator parameter translator, as the humanoid's is.

## Swimming

The fox swims with the humanoid's Swimming, tuned on its movement mode: its feet hang Swim Depth (a share of its height) below the surface, so its back is at the waterline and its raised head out of the water; it paddles at its Swim Speed (faster sprinting, Swim Sprint Speed), and jumps out (Swim Jump Height) onto edges low enough. It doesn't dive (diving is turned off on Fox Movement.asset: Swimming's Swim Dive Speed): a fox swims at the surface. The Swim and Swim Idle clips draw it at its standing height above its feet, as at the swim depth that puts it at the waterline; its paws aren't placed while it swims.

## Wall running

The fox wall-runs with the humanoid's WallRunning, tuned on its movement mode: its middle is held Wall Run Clearance out from the wall, a little beyond its side, and its runs start from lower jumps (Wall Run Min Height). Run at a wall at an angle and jump onto it, steering along it or on at it (as steeply as Wall Run Max Steer Angle lets a run start, moving along the wall at least Wall Run Min Speed): its long body meets the wall with its front end first, often while its side is still well off the wall, and it turns along the wall and is drawn in to it (see [Wall-running](abilities.md)). LegPlacementIK rolls its body toward the wall (about its legs' tops, along the way it runs) until its legs reach the wall (held near the wall, it rolls a little), as far as Max Wall Roll, easing in and out over Wall Roll Blend Time, and puts each paw on the wall's face: one the gait would take through the wall goes onto it, and a planted one is drawn onto it, held out by its leg's Wall Clearance (how far its paw's mesh reaches beyond its contact). It plays its gaits as it runs (the Wall Run state).


## Paw placement

Humanoid IK (foot placement, hands on ledges) works through Unity's humanoid rig, so the fox has its own: LegPlacementIK, on its model prefab, plants its paws on slopes, stairs and bumps. The animations are authored for flat ground at its feet; after the Animator poses it each frame, LegPlacementIK:

- **lays its body along the ground.** It probes the ground at points along the body (Body Probe Reach ahead of and behind its middle) and fits a line through them, then rests the line on the highest of them, so on stairs it runs along the steps' edges. The body is pitched to the line's slope (as far as Max Body Pitch) about its own middle, the point between its legs' tops, and raised or lowered so its shoulders and hips stand as high above the line as the animation has them stand above the floor. Up or down a ramp or a flight of stairs, the fox leans with it, along it, rather than a step at a time. Its tail and neck are turned back against the pitch (Counter Pitch Bones): the head looks ahead rather than at the sky or its paws, and climbing, the tail is held up off the slope behind; descending, it follows the body, up off the steps behind, and its tip is kept clear of the steps rising behind it (the Tail1 bone's Ground Clearance: turned up as far as that takes);
- **lowers an end of its body** as far as a planted paw there must reach further down than the animation reaches (a hind paw on a low tread drops the hips, not the shoulders), no further than Max Body Drop, and never so far that its shoulders or hips come nearer the ground under them than Leg Top Clearance;
- **bends each leg** (a two-bone solve of its upper and lower bones) to put its paw on the ground under it. That's the highest ground under its toes, under its joint (the wrist, or the hind leg's hock), and under the leg's middle joint where that leans out ahead of the paw (a hind leg's knee over a landing's edge as its paw leaves the last step). Each leg's middle joint always bends the way it folds (its Bend Direction): a front leg's elbow back, a hind leg's stifle forward. It keeps the animation's lift, so swinging paws clear the ground, and the paw's animated turn, tilted to the slope while planted. A swinging paw looks a little ahead of its toes (Swing Look Ahead) for a step up, higher ground than its slope carries on to, and is lifted onto it, so up a flight of stairs it comes up over the next step's edge rather than through its riser;
- **locks planted paws** (Foot Lock): as the animation puts a paw down, where it was put down is remembered (relative to the ground under it, so it rides moving ground), and the paw is held there until the animation lifts it, or the animation's paw gets further from it than Max Lock Distance; the lock blends in and out over Lock Blend Time. The model turns after the character, about its feet, which would otherwise drag planted paws round with it, a long body's furthest of all, and any mismatch between a stride and the fox's speed slides them. A held paw stays on the ground where it was put down, at the animation's lift above it, so on a slope it doesn't float or sink as the animation's paw moves on.

- **puts each paw on a foothold**: as a paw swings, where it'll be put down is chosen among where the animation has it and Foothold Reach ahead or behind, whichever has its ground nearest the body's line (so its leg is nearest the length the animation gives it), and not where its joints or toes would be in the ground there (a hind paw by the riser behind it, going down a flight, would have its hock in the step above; a paw at the back of a tread, going up, its toes in the riser ahead). On even ground every foothold is as good, and the paw goes where the animation has it. Each paw also moves with its leg's top as the body pitches, so the legs keep the upright stance the animation gives them;
- **shortens and quickens its strides on a slope** (Stride Shortening, down to Shortest Stride): a planted paw stays put while the body goes on over it the length of a stance, rising or falling as far along the slope, more than a leg can stretch or fold to. Its paws are drawn in toward its legs' tops, and the Animator played faster by as much while it moves (so the paws keep pace with the ground);
- **bends its body into a turn** (Turn Bend Bones: the spine, neck and head toward it, the tail away, so its tip swings round too), by how fast the model turns, as far as Max Turn Bend at Full Bend Turn Speed. Turning standing, its Animator blends from Idle into Turn Step (stepping in place) by its TurnSpeed, and its paws, lifted and locked again where the turning body puts them down, step round.

The ground the paws stand on is found where it's drawn: a moving platform is drawn between its physics steps as the fox is, while a ray hits it at its latest step, so each hit on one is moved to where it's drawn, and each paw's eased ground is carried along with what it stands on. Riding a platform up and down, the paws and body stay steady on it.

A paw counts as planted while the animation has it low (within Planted Height of the lowest it's had it lately: a hind paw rolls a little onto its toes through its stance) and isn't carrying it forward, relative to the body, faster than Swing Speed: a planted paw goes back as the body passes over it, a swinging one forward, however low a hind paw swings with its toes curled down.

The ground is probed from inside the character's body (Probe Height above its feet), where nothing else can be, so a probe never starts inside a step. The body's line is found each frame, in the world, so it climbs a flight as smoothly as the flight rises, though the character's feet jump a step at a time; only how far its ends are lowered is eased (Body Smooth Time), as is each paw's ground, in the world (Foot Smooth Time), never into it. Placement fades out in the air (over Grounded Fade Time), so jumps play as animated.

The fox's long, flat-bottomed body rests on the step under its front end going up a flight and its back end going down, so its middle is well above the flight and the paws at its other end well below its feet: the ground is looked for down to Max Step Down below them.

The fox's model prefab carries LegPlacementIK as Prepare Generic Rig Models set it up, from its setup (see [Its setup](#its-setup)). Its other settings are LegPlacementIK's defaults, since the model prefab is made anew each time it's prepared: tune them on the component's defaults, or on the prefab after preparing. A model stepped by hand (the tests do) calls its `Solve` after each `Animator.Update`; in the game it runs in LateUpdate while the Animator plays.

**On another generic-rig character.** LegPlacementIK isn't the fox's own: Prepare Generic Rig Models gives it to every generic-rig character, from its setup's Root Bone and Legs, Counter Pitch Bones and Turn Bend Bones. To set one up by hand, or from code of your own: put it on the model prefab (with the Animator) and give it its Root Bone and its Legs (for each, its Upper, Lower and Foot bones, its Bend Direction and its Wall Clearance). From code, `SetLegs(root, legs)` sets both, then `SetContact(leg, point)` puts each paw's contact (given in the model's space with the model in its bind pose); `SetCounterPitchBones` and `SetTurnBendBones` set the bones turned back against the pitch and bent into a turn. `GenericRigModelImporter.AddPawPlacement` (in `Assets/MeridianMotion/Editor/Models/GenericRigModelImporter.Build.cs`) is the worked example.

## Generating the fox (the model pipeline)

The fox's generator isn't part of the package, and needs Blender (or the Fox.fbx already in the project, to rebuild from). It's the model pipeline's, with the mannequin's (see [The mannequin and its clips](animation.md#the-mannequin-and-its-clips-the-model-pipeline)): `FoxGenerator`, in Assets/Showcase/Editor/Pipeline/GenericRigs (the pipeline's own editor assembly, `MeridianMotion.Showcase.Pipeline`), and its Blender scripts in Tools/Fox. Without it, the fox's assets in the project play as they are.

Tools > Meridian Motion > Showcase > Generate Fox (or `-executeMethod MeridianMotion.Showcase.Pipeline.FoxGenerator.GenerateFromCommandLine`, with `-skipBlender` to rebuild from the Fox.fbx that's there) runs `Tools/Fox/generate_fox.py` in Blender (found as Generate Mannequin finds it, by `BlenderRunner`: see [Finding Blender](animation.md#finding-blender); its scripts share Tools/Blender/blender_common.py with the mannequin's), which builds, rigs, skins and animates the fox (its clips in `Tools/Fox/fox_animations.py`). Everything after that is the package's generic-rig path: the generator sets up its clips' import again (`GenericRigModelImporter.ConfigureImport`), then prepares it from its setup as any generic-rig character is (see [A generic rig of your own](#a-generic-rig-of-your-own)), its Animator Controller from its layout (`Fox Layout.asset`: see [Animator layouts](animation.md#animator-layouts)), and saves Fox.prefab as New Character saves a generic-rig character's (`NewCharacter.SaveVariant`). Its setup, body and movement mode are made only if they aren't there, and left as they're edited. Run the script by hand with `--preview <folder>` to render it from the side, front and three-quarters, and from the side through each clip.

A fox's legs fold as a dog's do: a front leg's elbow points back (lifting, the forearm flexes forward and the wrist folds the paw back under it), a hind leg's stifle forward. Each leg flexes only at its middle joint (elbow or stifle) to keep its paw from going below the ground, and always the way it folds, keeping the line from its shoulder or hip to its paw. A joint bent one way on one key and the other on the next snaps across between them, sweeping the paw through the floor for a frame. The clips are keyed densely (`FPS` in fox_animations.py), so a galloping leg landing straight and flexing fast stays off the floor between keys too.

The gaits are authored for speeds (`SPEEDS` in fox_animations.py) that the fox's layout's Locomotion thresholds match (`Fox Layout.asset`: see [Animator layouts](animation.md#animator-layouts)), so the paws keep to the ground at them; change both together.

### Writing its setup

The generator knows the fox's bones (generate_fox.py names them), so it writes the fox's setup itself, where it's missing, rather than guessing it: its legs, each paw's contact at its toes (generate_fox.py's toe joints, which its bones don't record: they end at the paws' joints; the toes stay down through a stride while the paw rolls over them), its legs' bend directions and wall clearances, its tail and neck turned back against the pitch, its spine, neck, head and tail bent into turns, and its hitbox bones (its muzzle and ears go to its head's, its hind paws to its hind feet's). After that, `Fox Setup.asset` is a source to edit, as its body is.
