# Character Model and Animations

This page is in two parts. The first, up to [IK](#ik), is the models, animation and IK the package has: the shared Animator Controller and what it plays, replacing the model, preparing models (Prepare Character Models, which needs no Blender), the animation libraries and the IK. The second, [The mannequin and its clips (the model pipeline)](#the-mannequin-and-its-clips-the-model-pipeline), is how the mannequin, its clips and the shared controller are made, in Blender; it isn't part of the package.

## Models, animation and IK

The character is a mannequin, rigged as a Unity Humanoid (generated by the model pipeline: see [Generate Mannequin](#generate-mannequin)). It walks, jogs, sprints and jumps with Quaternius's Universal Animation Libraries (see [Quaternius animations](#quaternius-animations)). It stands, walks briskly, runs and dashes with procedurally keyframed animations of its own: a still, evenly balanced stance, the gaits between the libraries' walk and jog, and a dash. An Animator (the Animator Controller every model shares, Assets/Animation/Mannequin.controller) blends between them using the character's speed, vertical speed, grounded and dashing state, and a Jump trigger.

Crouching, sliding, rolling, wall-running, wall climbing, hanging, climbing, vaulting, pushing, swinging on a rope, riding a zipline and swimming each have a state of their own. Each is entered from any state while the character does it (a Bool parameter of the same name), and left for whichever of standing, crouching or the air matches the character afterwards. They play the libraries' crouch (still, and crouch-walking by speed), slide, roll, climb onto a ledge, wall climbing loops, safety vault and push:

- The roll, the climb and the vault play through once over however long each takes (the RollProgress, ClimbProgress and VaultProgress parameters drive their time).
- The push plays at the speed the character pushes, and backward when it pulls (GrabSpeed).
- A wall run plays the library's wall run for the side the wall is on, at the run's speed (the sprint, without the libraries).
- A bounce off a bounce pad plays the jump (the Jump trigger fires on Bounced as on jumps).
- Swimming plays the library's treading water, blending into its swim stroke as the character swims faster (see [Swimming](#swimming)). A vault out of the water plays the climb onto a ledge instead of the vault (`AnimationController` shows it as climbing, at the vault's progress), starting part way into the climb (the AnimationController's Climb Out Of Water Start): a swimmer at the edge is already higher than one hanging from it, so the climb's start dropped the body before it rose.

Some states are poses of the mannequin's own, since the libraries have nothing for them:

- Hanging from a ledge and sliding down a wall, checked against the Quaternius library's climbs (which have no hang or wall slide of their own): the hands grip about as far apart as theirs do, at head height on a wall, and the feet rest on the wall in front of the hips. On a ledge, LedgeHandIK puts the hands on it.
- Hanging from a rope (Rope Swing: one hand high and one lower, the knees drawn up together) and from a zipline's cable (Zipline: both hands together overhead).
- A crouch, for when the libraries aren't installed. Without them (see [Without the libraries](#without-the-libraries)), a slide shows the dash, a roll the tuck at the top of a jump, a climb or a vault the rise, and a push the walk.

Foot placement fades out while sliding, rolling and vaulting, as it does dashing.

## Walking and running

The Locomotion blend tree plays, by speed, each loop at the speed its strides are made for, measured on the mannequin when the controller was built (see [Building the clips and controller](#building-the-clips-and-controller)), so its planted feet stay put there. From slowest to fastest:

| Animation | Where its speed comes from |
|---|---|
| the stance (the mannequin's) | standing still |
| Quaternius's walk | its measured strides |
| the mannequin's brisk walk | its measured strides, then shown a little past them (`BriskWalkReach` times its own speed, played up to `BriskWalkPlayback` times faster: Assets/Editor/Pipeline/Mannequin/MannequinGenerator.cs), so a walk a little faster than people naturally walk still looks like a walk |
| the mannequin's run | its measured strides |
| Quaternius's jog (sprinting) | its measured strides |
| Quaternius's sprint | its measured strides: the end of the tree (see below) |

**Faster than the fastest loop.** No blend tree has a top speed fixed when it's built, which a retuned character could outrun. Each speed-driven state (Locomotion, Crouch and Wall Climb) ends at its fastest loop's own speed, and past it the AnimatorParameterTranslator plays that loop faster, by however much faster the character moves, so its feet keep up with the ground at any speed: a dash's leftover momentum, a faster run, a retuned climb. The controller carries the speed each fastest loop is made for (its TopSpeed parameters' defaults, written by Generate Mannequin), and the translator sets each state's playback multiplier (the Playback past the fastest loop section of the translator asset). A controller without them plays as Unity blend trees do, holding at their last loop's speed.

Each loop is offset to land its left foot at the same moment (TouchDownPhase, measured on the mannequin), so blending between them never mixes one foot's stride with the other's. The movement's walk and run speeds are tuned to suit these loops, so retune them together (see [Walking and Sprinting](movement.md#walking-and-sprinting)).

## Jumping and landing

Jumping plays Quaternius's jump start from its take-off (the Jump trigger, fired by the Jumped and AirJumped movement events), skipping the crouch before it, since the character has already left the ground. Then it plays their jump loop while in the air, and their landing as soon as the character is grounded and no longer rising. Moving on (faster than `MoveOnFromLanding` in MannequinGenerator.cs) cuts the landing short. Every clip shares one floor (see [Building the clips and controller](#building-the-clips-and-controller)).

The Animator is given a vertical speed of 0 while the character touches the ground, where its vertical movement only follows the ground. Walking up stairs lifts the character quickly at each step, which used to keep a character that landed on a staircase on the run in its jump all the way up.

## Swimming

Swimming underwater (the Diving parameter, set with Swimming) has a state of its own, Swim Underwater, so a controller can give it its own clips. The generated one plays the library's swim stroke alone, never its treading water: underwater the body lies flat, so drifting without swimming it strokes slowly (at the generator's UnderwaterDriftStrokePace), rather than treading upright with its legs hanging below the body (through the floor, near the bottom). Without the library, it plays the same as Swim. It's entered from any state while diving, and swimming back at the surface returns to Swim. Underwater the body lies on the character's feet while the swimming clips float it Swim Depth above them, so the AnimationController draws the model lowered by the movement's model drop (`MovementData.modelDrop`), easing to it (and back up at the surface) over Model Drop Smooth Time. Pitched underwater (see [Pitch](movement.md#collision)), the model is tilted with the body about its feet, lowered along the body's up, so it stays inside it.

The Swim state plays Quaternius's treading water still, blending by Speed into their swim stroke, shown fully from `SwimStrokeSpeed` (in MannequinGenerator.cs) up: the stroke is on the spot, with no strides to measure, so it's shown at any swimming speed past that.

The swimming clips are floated for the Character prefab's Swim Depth (Swimming's, on its movement mode), so after retuning it, run Generate Mannequin again, or the swimmer floats too high or too low (see [Floating the swimming clips](#floating-the-swimming-clips)).

## Replacing the model
The model and its animations are kept separate from the character's movement, so they can be replaced without touching the character.

**CharacterModelSource** is a ScriptableObject that gives the character its model. The character's CharacterModelHost calls its `CreateModelAsync(context, cancellationToken)` at runtime, which creates the model under the character, sets it up and hands it back as a `CharacterModel` (the instance, its Animator, IK and translator). It also holds the settings that apply however a model is stored: the translator that animates it, optional Animator Controller and Avatar overrides, placement, and whether to add IK to humanoids.

- To give the character a new look, assign a different source to the Character prefab's CharacterModelHost (or a scene's instance of it).
- Its Height setting either keeps the model's own size (times Scale) or fits it to its Fit Height, for the character at its authored size. The character's own size multiplies either, so on a character twice the size, a fitted model is twice its Fit Height. Previews and the Character Model window's heights are sized the same way.

**DirectCharacterModelSource** (Create > Character Showcase > Character Model (Direct Reference)) is the source that references its model directly: a prefab or model file, instantiated with `Object.InstantiateAsync` so even a heavy model doesn't stall the game. Prepare Character Models makes one for every model it sets up.

**Other sources** subclass CharacterModelSource. One that loads from Addressables or an asset bundle overrides `CreateModelAsync` to load and instantiate the model, then calls `SetUpModelAsync` to apply the shared settings, and overrides `ReleaseModel` to release the instance (`Addressables.ReleaseInstance`) rather than destroy it. `SetUpModelAsync` is virtual and awaitable too, for applying a character creator's choices. If the cancellation token fires (the character was destroyed or given another model first), a source cleans up whatever it has loaded and throws `OperationCanceledException`; awaiting `Awaitable.NextFrameAsync(cancellationToken)` and similar does that.

**CharacterModelHost** owns the character's model.

- It loads it at runtime, in its Start, when Load Model On Start is on (otherwise code loads one with `LoadModelAsync`, or the character goes without: a server's, say), so it never stalls the game, and the character moves normally while it loads. Before that, at Start, it fits the body the model will want from what its source measured when it was prepared (`FitBodyFromSource`; see Body Fit below), so the character moves its final body from its first step, with its model or without one.
- `await CharacterModelHost.LoadModelAsync(source)` swaps models at runtime, returning whether the new model loaded. New models are created hidden, under an inactive staging transform, and swapped in the same frame the old one goes, so two models are never seen at once; the old one goes back to the source that made it with `ReleaseModel`.
- While there's no model it shows a placeholder (a capsule the size of the character's body), and if a model fails to load it loads its fallback source (the mannequin) instead.
- It keeps an Eye Point child at the model's eye height (from its eye bones, or partway up its head), which the camera's first-person view follows.
- With Body Fit off (below), it warns if a model is taller than the character's body, or shorter than its Minimum Height Fraction of it. With Body Fit on, the body is fitted to the model instead, so there's nothing to warn about.
- `CharacterModelHost.Hitboxes` is the model's hitboxes (see Hitboxes, below).
- Its Body Fit setting can instead fit the character's body to each model as it's swapped in. Height scales the body as authored to be as tall as the model, keeping its proportions. Model's Body gives it the model's own body: its source's Body Definition (see below), sized as the source sizes the model, which can be of another kind than the character's body as authored (a box for a capsule: the body is replaced with one of its kind). For a source without one, it's fitted to the model as it loads, as Prepare Character Models fits one (as wide as the model's shoulders times the host's Body Width Margin). The character's size still multiplies the fitted body, and like growing, fitting a bigger one waits until there's room. Off leaves the body as authored: the MovementController's Body Definition asset, where it's tuned. Off is the component's default, but the Character prefab sets Model's Body, so the humanoid takes its model's body (`[name] Body.asset` beside its source); Fox.prefab turns it off, keeping its own hand-tuned body (see [Creatures](creatures.md)).

AnimationController hears about new models through the host's `ModelLoaded` event and animates them; it no longer handles models itself.

### Hitboxes
Each model has hitboxes (`CharacterHitboxes`, on the model): a capsule on each part of its body (a `Hitbox`: the hips, spine, chest, neck and head, and each arm's and leg's three bones, for a humanoid; the fox's are listed in `FoxGenerator.HitboxBones`), moving with its bones as it's animated, on the CharacterHitbox layer. They're what meets the world for the character, which has no collider of its own: its movement moves its body, a shape (see [Collision](movement.md#collision)), and never meets them, nor do its feet's IK or the camera (they leave the layer out, or ignore triggers). A model swapped in brings its own.

- **Response** says how they meet what comes at the character. Detect (as they're made) makes them triggers: what passes into them is reported, and goes on through. Collide makes them solid: what meets them bounces off, and the character pushes physics objects with its limbs.
- **HitStarted** and **HitEnded** report what starts and stops meeting one (with which hitbox). Only what moves (has a Rigidbody) is reported, unless Report Static Contacts is on: the feet and hands brush floors and walls all the time.
- **Fitting.** Fit Hitboxes (Tools > Character Showcase > Fit Hitboxes) fits them to every model, and Prepare Character Models and Generate Fox to each model they save, keeping its settings. Each capsule covers the part of the mesh weighted most to its bone (and to the bones under it that have none of their own: a hand's fingers go to the hand), in the bind pose: along whichever way makes it smallest, trimmed a little inside the skin. Hitbox Bones lists the bones to give one, by name, if not the usual ones. Refit rather than edit them by hand: fitting replaces them.

A model swapped in mid-move carries on where the old one was, rather than starting over from standing. Just before the swap, `ModelReplacing` lets the old model's animation driver record its state (`Retire`), and the new model's driver takes it up (`ContinueFrom`). For an Animator, that's its parameters, and each layer's weight, state, how far through it, and any transition it was part-way through, matched by name, so variants of one controller carry over. Foot placement carries over too, so planted feet stay planted rather than fading back in. A translator for another animation system can do the same by implementing those two methods; by default they do nothing.

**CharacterAnimationState** is the layer between movement and animation: velocity (world and relative to facing), speeds, turn rate, grounded (with a short grace period for small step-offs), time in the air, moving, jumping and dashing; and each ability's own state (crouching, hanging and the ledge it hangs from, a vault's or a climb's progress, the ladder's rungs, the rope or cable gripped, swimming and diving), the time the animation update covers (`deltaTime`, which the IK eases by: see [IK](#ik)) and the character's scale. Animation code reads this rather than the movement system's data. Components on a model, like FootPlacementIK, find it through `ICharacterAnimationStateSource` in their parents, so they work on any character.

**Movement events** are raised by `MovementController.MovementEventRaised` after each physics step, one for each that happened in it: Jumped, AirJumped, Landed, LeftGround, HitCeiling and HitWall, and each ability's start and end (DashStarted and DashEnded, SlideStarted, LedgeGrabbed, WallRunEnded, SwimStarted and so on); `MovementEvents` lists them all. The movement's components report the ones they cause (jumps, and each ability's start and end, declared by the component that sets its state), and the controller detects the rest. `AnimationController.MovementEventReceived` passes them on with the animation state, for effects and sound.

**CharacterAnimationTranslator** (a ScriptableObject) turns the animation state and events into a particular model's animations. The included **AnimatorParameterTranslator** sets Animator parameters you name (speed, vertical, forward and sideways speed, turn speed, grounded, jumping, dashing, moving) and fires triggers on chosen movement events, skipping any the controller doesn't have. A different animation system can be supported by writing another translator.

In the editor, where the model isn't loaded, the CharacterModelHost draws it as a gizmo so the character can still be seen and placed.

## Character models
Character models live in subfolders of Assets/Models/CharacterModels: the generated mannequin, Quaternius's female and male mannequins (CC0, from the Universal Animation Library), and a custom model, the Meridian Complex suit (MeridianComplexSuit/base_basic_shaded). Any rigged humanoid model can be added the same way. Every model plays the mannequin's animations through Humanoid retargeting. Three tools under Tools > Character Showcase manage them: Prepare Character Models, Fit Hitboxes (see [Hitboxes](#hitboxes)) and Set Up Scene. The mannequin itself is made by a fourth, Generate Mannequin, the model pipeline's (see [Generate Mannequin](#generate-mannequin)).

**Prepare Character Models** imports and sets up every model file in Assets/Models/CharacterModels.

- It makes each a Humanoid, and straightens its arms and legs into a T-pose for the avatar's reference pose, so models rigged in an A-pose don't hold their arms up in every animation.
- It saves a model prefab next to each model ("[name] Model.prefab", with its Animator, CharacterIK, FootPlacementIK, LedgeHandIK, WallSlideIK, LadderIK and LineGripIK) and a DirectCharacterModelSource ("[name].asset").
- It records the model's height on its source, unscaled (Measured Own Height; with the source's sizing, `MeasuredHeight`), and fits the model its own body (`BodyFitting`), written as `[name] Body.asset` beside the source and set as its Body Definition, in the model's own units. A humanoid's is an upright capsule as tall as it is and as wide as its shoulders (times `BodyFitting.DefaultShoulderMargin`, for the arms), lying as thick as the model is deep in its bind pose (when that's thinner than it's wide); a model without shoulders would get a box its size. A body asset with Refit On Prepare off is left as it is, for one shaped by hand. A character that fits its body to its model (the CharacterModelHost's Body Fit) takes it, so what a character with a model can do can be worked out before one's spawned (`CharacterModelHost.BodyFor`; see [Capabilities](movement.md#capabilities)).
- A folder can hold several model files, such as variants of one model; each is its own choice, and its prefab and source are always named after its file, so adding or removing other models never renames them.
- Models that aren't rigged, or whose bones Unity can't map to a Humanoid, are reported and skipped. Each saved prefab is read back from disk and checked for its avatar and the shared Animator Controller, so a model is only reported ready, and listed in the window, once it can actually animate.
- Models whose units were lost (a character hundreds of metres tall, or a few centimetres), outside the range `MinHeight` to `MaxHeight` in CharacterModelImporter.cs, get their source scaled to `TypicalHeight` when it's first made (and a warning saying so). A source that's already there keeps the Scale it has, so a change made to it by hand isn't undone.
- IK settings and edits to the model sources are kept.

It runs by itself: when model files in the folder are added, changed, moved or deleted, or animation they play changes (an Animator Controller, a clip, or a model source naming a different controller), it prepares whichever are new or changed once the editor is idle (a new model is imported as a Humanoid from the start), and skips the rest. The menu item prepares them all regardless. Prepared assets found under other names (an older naming scheme, or a renamed file) are moved to the current names, keeping their references, and sources whose model file is gone are reported rather than deleted.

**Set Up Scene…** opens a window for choosing which character, model (any Prepare Character Models has set up, with previews and heights) and movement mode a scene spawns, and sets the open scene up to spawn it (see [Spawning the Character](spawning.md)). The choice is stored on the scene's Character Spawner, so the Character prefab itself, which the tests spawn, keeps the mannequin.

All three also run from the command line for automation (each quits Unity when it's done, with code 1 if it failed, so `-quit` isn't needed): `-executeMethod CharacterShowcase.EditorTools.CharacterModelImporter.PrepareAllFromCommandLine`, `...HitboxFitter.FitAllFromCommandLine`, and `...SceneGameSetup.SetUpFromCommandLine -scene Assets/Scenes/LevelPlayground/LevelPlayground.unity -model "base_basic_shaded"` (see [Spawning the Character](spawning.md)).

### The reference pose

A Humanoid avatar's reference pose decides how every clip looks on that model, so Prepare Character Models builds it carefully. It starts from the skin's bind pose, the pose the mesh was modelled in, rather than the pose the skeleton is saved in, which can be an animation frame. Unity takes an FBX's default pose from its first take, and the mannequin's used to be the dash, so every mannequin clip was stored relative to a forward lean and other models played them leaning far back. (The mannequin now exports a one-frame rest-pose take first, which isn't imported as a clip.) It then straightens the spine up, the arms out and the legs down. With that, every model holds its torso at the mannequin's lean and keeps its planted feet in place to within a few percent of its speed, with no per-model tuning.

### Feet on the ground at any size

Two more steps keep every model's feet on the ground at any size.

First, the axis conversion. Blender's FBX export rotates the armature node about X to convert axes. The Animator poses that node as the avatar stores it and places the body through it, so when the character was stretched along one axis only, the rotation turned the stretch onto another axis: a taller character had its hips raised out of proportion and its feet lifted off the ground. The reference pose is now saved with nodes above the skeleton unrotated (and everything under them where it was), as models without such a node have it. A node that's also unevenly scaled has its scale's axes swapped with the turn, which keeps everything under it exactly as it was for turns of whole quarter turns (any axis conversion). One turned any other way can't be straightened without shearing what's under it, so it's left and reported. (An uneven scale on its own doesn't distort a stretched model, so it isn't reported.)

Then, the measurements. The model's soles are measured standing in its idle clip, with its feet where foot placement holds them, and saved as its source's Ground Offset, by which the model is lowered so its soles, not its origin, rest on the ground.

- Its soles are also measured with its feet where its animation puts them, as its Animated Ground Offset. On a model of other proportions than the clips' own, the two differ slightly, and foot placement raises or lowers the body by the difference, in proportion to how far it's faded out (in the air, dashing, or easing in on landing), so the soles rest on the floor whether it's on or off. (While it's partly on, over the Grounded Fade Time on FootPlacementIK, Unity's IK blends the legs' joint rotations, which dips the feet very slightly.)
- The way the body faces as it stands is measured too. Humanoid animation keeps the facing a model was made with (though it centres the body on the model's origin, however far off it was made), so a model made facing away from +Z gets a Facing Correction, to the nearest quarter turn, that turns it to face forward.
- Every mesh counts, including ones carried by a bone rather than skinned, such as shoes. Being inside the scaled model parent, the offset scales with the character.

Sources made by hand or in code, which haven't been measured, have Measure Ground On Load set, and measure their model the same way when it loads (StandingPose, in the runtime code, does the measuring both here and at import).

A source measures its model once, for every copy: the measurement is kept until its model, Animator Controller or Avatar changes, the source is edited, `ForgetMeasurement()` is called, or Play mode starts again. A source whose models may each differ (a character creator's, say) returns null from `ModelTemplate`, so each model is measured as it loads. A prepared model is measured again whenever the model, its controller or the clips those play change, since it depends on them: each model's record (in Library/CharacterShowcase/PreparedModels.txt) holds a hash of its file and of its controller with everything the controller depends on.

## Quaternius animations
Assets/Animation/Quaternius holds Quaternius's Universal Animation Library (its Pro edition) and Universal Animation Library 2 (its Source edition, a superset of the Standard one), from quaternius.com: hundreds of animations on one humanoid rig, CC0 (public domain; see LICENSE.txt there).

Besides the walk, jog, sprint and jump, the character uses their crouch, roll, slide, climb up, push, wall climbing loops and wall runs. There's a wall run for a wall on each side, chosen by the WallSide parameter, played at the character's speed against its strides as Generate Mannequin measures them. They also have wall jumps, a taller climb, a vault, double jumps, swimming, carrying, combat, interacting and more, ready for mechanics to come; the README there lists them.

Tools > Character Showcase > Prepare Quaternius Animations makes each a looping or one-shot Humanoid clip named for its take (Generate Mannequin runs it too). Their female and male mannequins (CC0 too) are in Assets/Models/CharacterModels/Quaternius as character models of their own. The pack ships the female one as a model file; the male one is the mannequin the libraries' animations are on, taken out of `UAL1.fbx` (see [The Blender scripts for clip libraries](#the-blender-scripts-for-clip-libraries)). The FBX files are stored with Git LFS, so run `git lfs install` once before cloning.

## Motion capture clips
Assets/Animation/CMU holds motion-capture clips from the CMU Graphics Lab Motion Capture Database (http://mocap.cs.cmu.edu), free to use, modify and redistribute but not to sell as animations (see LICENSE.txt there). The character no longer plays them (the Quaternius animations replaced its walk, run and jump), but they're kept for mechanics to come. They cover walking, running, jumping, stairs and steps, crouching, sneaking and crawling, sitting and getting up, climbing, carrying, pushing and pulling, picking things up, falls and idles, sorted into folders and named for what they show; CLIPS.md lists each with CMU's description. They're stored with Git LFS too.

They've been edited for a character controller, in Blender (see [The Blender scripts for clip libraries](#the-blender-scripts-for-clip-libraries)). Tools > Character Showcase > Prepare Motion Capture Clips then makes each a Humanoid animation with its own avatar (each performer had their own proportions), mapped by the CMU skeleton's bone names. Each starts after the T-pose frame, has its height based on the feet (so its floor is the model's own), and faces the way the body does, so every clip faces the character's forward whichever way it was captured.

MotionCaptureLocomotion can cut loops from them: it searches a take's steady middle for the two frames a cycle apart whose poses, and how they're moving, match best. It also measures any loop's stride speed, the moment it lands its left foot, a jump's take-off and landing, and a clip's lowest foot, which Generate Mannequin uses on every clip (see [Building the clips and controller](#building-the-clips-and-controller)).

Two things turned up fitting real motion capture to foot placement:

- On a model stretched along one axis, Unity reports foot goals scaled about the clip's root, so a clip whose height was kept as captured (the performer's floor, a little off the model's) had its feet planted too low on a stretched model. Based on the feet, the clip's floor is the model's, and they're on the ground.
- Unity's Foot IK option on a state, which would pin each model's feet to the clip's, has the same stretch problem, so it's left off.

## IK
CharacterIK (on each model, reachable via CharacterModelHost.IK) layers inverse kinematics over the animations. Hands and feet can be pulled to target positions and rotations, and elbows and knees bent toward hint positions. Each has its own 0-1 weight that fades smoothly in and out, blending with the animation underneath. Targets can be Transforms assigned in the inspector or positions set from code:

```csharp
ik.SetGoal(IKLimb.RightHand, doorHandle.position, doorHandle.rotation); // reach for a handle
ik.SetHint(IKLimb.LeftFoot, kneeTarget.position, 0.5f);                 // push the left knee outward
ik.ClearGoal(IKLimb.RightHand);                                         // fade back to the animation
```

To try manual targets in the editor, right-click the CharacterIK component and choose "Create Target Transforms For All Limbs", then enter Play mode, raise a weight, and drag the target around in the Scene view.

On a model of other proportions than the clips were made for, Unity reports each hand's and foot's goal where the clip's own character had it, which can be a few centimetres from where this model has it (most for the Meridian Complex suit's hands). So a goal set to where the animation reports it moves the hand or foot there, and a goal at partial weight is blended from there. A goal at full weight lands where it's set.

The IK that places limbs for the character derives from `AbilityIK`, which solves in CharacterIK's BeforeSolve, reads the animation state from the model's parents, and eases its placement in and out (each component's own weight and fade time):

| Component | What it places | Described in |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FootPlacementIK | the feet on the ground | [Foot placement](#foot-placement) |
| LedgeHandIK | the hands on a ledge hung from, and the planted hand on what's vaulted | [Ledges](abilities.md#ledges), [Vaulting](abilities.md#vaulting) |
| WallSlideIK | the hands on a wall slid down, the body against a wall climbed, the feet on a wall run along | [Wall Sliding](abilities.md#wall-sliding), [Climbing animations](traversal.md#climbing-animations), [Wall-Running](abilities.md#wall-running) |
| LadderIK | the hands and feet on a ladder's rungs | [Ladders](traversal.md#ladders) |
| LineGripIK | the hands on a rope or a zipline's cable | [Ropes and ziplines](#ropes-and-ziplines) |

The fox's PawPlacementIK isn't one of them: it poses its Generic rig itself (see [Creatures](creatures.md#paw-placement)).

**Timing.** The IK eases by the time of the Animator update it's solved in (`CharacterAnimationStates.DeltaTimeOf`), not always the frame's. An Animator that updates itself does so by the frame's time (or the physics step's, or the unscaled frame's, for those update modes), and the IK eases by the same. One turned off and stepped by hand (`Animator.Update`, as the tests do, or a replay) is fed its animation state for each step, and the IK eases by that state's `deltaTime`, so it keeps pace with the steps rather than with however long the render frame was.

### On stretched models
Unity's humanoid IK gets stretched models (scaled more on one axis than another: see [Character Size](movement.md#character-size)) wrong in two ways, and CharacterIK corrects both:
- It reports foot goals as if the model were scaled evenly by its width, so a model stretched taller had its feet placed below the floor, and one stretched shorter above it.
- It solves each limb from where the limb starts (the hip joint, or the collarbone), moving the hand or foot further than asked across and along the model, by the model's width and depth scale over its height scale.

With the corrections, hands and feet land on their goals on every model and stretch (`AnimationTests.MovingAGoal_MovesTheHandOrFootAsFar_AtAnyStretch` holds the tolerances).

### Foot placement

FootPlacementIK uses the same system to plant the feet on slopes, stairs and bumps automatically. It raycasts under each animated foot, shifts the foot to the real ground while keeping the animation's foot lift, tilts planted feet to the slope, and lowers the pelvis so the lower foot can reach. It feeds a per-frame procedural channel underneath the manual goals, so targets set from code still take priority. To try it, run Tools > Character Showcase > Create Foot Placement Test Area to add stairs, ramps and bumps in front of the character.

Foot placement also locks planted feet. As the animation plants a foot, where it was put down is remembered (relative to the ground under it, so it rides moving ground), and an IK goal blended over the animation holds it there until the animation lifts it, or the animation's foot gets further from it than FootPlacementIK's Max Lock Distance. The model turns after the character about its feet (see [Turning and Leaning](movement.md#turning-and-leaning)), which would otherwise drag planted feet round with it: turning on the spot, they slid without locking, and don't with it. Foot Lock on FootPlacementIK sets how firmly (0 turns it off).

A model with a Generic rig, which Unity's humanoid IK can't pose, places its feet another way: the fox's PawPlacementIK pitches its body with the ground and bends its legs itself (see [Creatures](creatures.md#paw-placement)).

### Ropes and ziplines

LineGripIK, on every model, puts the hands on the rope or the cable while swinging or riding, moves the body up or down so the hands reach (up to its Max Body Shift), and eases in and out.

- On a rope, the right hand goes where the character caught hold and the left below it (Rope Hand Gap), the body hanging back behind the rope (Rope Body Back) and leaning with it, about the hands, as it swings (Rope Lean).
- On a zipline, both hands go on the cable overhead, a little apart along it (Cable Hand Gap).

## The mannequin and its clips (the model pipeline)

This part isn't in the package, and needs Blender (though most of it can rebuild from the files already made). It's the model pipeline: the generators and `BlenderRunner` in Assets/Editor/Pipeline (Mannequin, Creatures and Blender: their own editor assembly, `CharacterShowcase.Pipeline.Editor`), and the Blender scripts in Tools/Mannequin, Tools/Fox, Tools/Blender and Tools/Animation. Without it, the mannequin, its clips and the shared Animator Controller in the project are used as they are, and Prepare Character Models prepares models as before. The fox's generator is described with the fox (see [Generating the fox](creatures.md#generating-the-fox-the-model-pipeline)).

### Generate Mannequin

**Generate Mannequin** (Tools > Character Showcase > Generate Mannequin) runs Tools/Mannequin/generate_mannequin.py in Blender to build the mannequin's mesh, rig and animation clips (its own clips are keyframed in Tools/Mannequin/mannequin_animations.py). It then:

- imports them as looping, in-place Humanoid clips;
- builds the Animator Controller every model shares (Assets/Animation/Mannequin.controller, rebuilt in place so the models' references to it never break) and its translator;
- prepares the mannequin like any other model (its file and model prefab are where `CharacterModelImporter.MannequinModelPath` and `MannequinPrefabPath` say), and makes it the Character prefab's model.

If Blender isn't available, it can rebuild everything from the Mannequin.fbx already in the project. To change the mannequin or its animations, edit generate_mannequin.py or mannequin_animations.py and run this again. From the command line: `-executeMethod CharacterShowcase.EditorTools.MannequinGenerator.GenerateFromCommandLine` (with `-blender <path>` or `-skipBlender`; it quits Unity when it's done, with code 1 if it failed).

When Mannequin.fbx changes some other way (regenerated with Blender by hand, say), the rest is rebuilt from it by itself: before Prepare Character Models' own run prepares changed models (`CharacterModelPostprocessor.BeforePreparing`), the pipeline regenerates the mannequin's clips and controller from the file if it has changed since it was prepared (`MannequinRegeneration`). Without the pipeline, nothing is regenerated there.

### Finding Blender

Generate Mannequin finds Blender through `BlenderRunner` (Assets/Editor/Pipeline/Blender/BlenderRunner.cs), which Generate Fox uses too: where Blender was last located (remembered in the editor's preferences) or a usual install place, and otherwise it asks once, offering to rebuild from the FBX that's there instead. From the command line, `-blender <path>` names it and `-skipBlender` rebuilds from the FBX. The Blender scripts share what both generators need (posing helpers, materials, the skinning check, reading their arguments, and the FBX export written the way Unity imports it) in Tools/Blender/blender_common.py.

### Building the clips and controller

Generate Mannequin measures each locomotion loop's stride speed when it builds the controller (MotionCaptureLocomotion's StrideSpeed), which the Locomotion blend tree's thresholds are (see [Walking and running](#walking-and-running)), and logs it ("[MannequinGenerator] Walk_Loop: strides made for … m/s").

The libraries' clips are raised or lowered so their lowest foot stands where the mannequin's stance does (their root height offsets, set by Generate Mannequin, which logs them), so every clip shares one floor. The landing's crouch still puts the soles a few millimetres off it, as the libraries' proportions differ a little from the mannequin's.

#### Floating the swimming clips

The library's swimming is made around the water line: where a grounded clip has its floor, its swimmer has the water's surface. A swimming character's origin is still its feet, hanging Swimming's Swim Depth below the surface, so Generate Mannequin raises the swimming clips (their root height offsets, which it logs), measured on the mannequin with its feet the Character prefab's Swim Depth below the surface: treading water so its head stands `HeadAboveWater` above the surface (the chin just out of the water), and the stroke so its hips are `HipsBelowWater` under it (the back just breaking the surface). The Swim Depth is the movement's tuning, so after changing it, run Generate Mannequin again, or the swimmer floats too high or too low. Without the libraries, the Swim state is the mannequin's own treading water, made for the default Swim Depth.

### Without the libraries

Without the libraries in the project, Generate Mannequin uses the mannequin's own clips throughout:

- a walk, the brisk walk, run and sprint (a gait that keeps its planted feet from sliding: each foot's pivot moves straight back at the sprint's speed while down, reached by inverse kinematics);
- rising, a tuck at the top and falling, blended by vertical speed.

### The Blender scripts for clip libraries

The scripts in Tools/Animation edit the animation libraries in Blender:

- extract_mannequin.py takes Quaternius's male mannequin (the one the libraries' animations are on) out of `UAL1.fbx`, without its animations. Run it again with Blender to remake it (`Blender -b --factory-startup --python Tools/Animation/extract_mannequin.py -- Assets/Animation/Quaternius/UAL1.fbx Assets/Models/CharacterModels/Quaternius/Quaternius_Mannequin_M.fbx`), then Prepare Character Models.
- remove_root_motion.py takes the hips' travel across the ground out of each motion-capture clip, keeping their height (jumps still rise) and the sway within each step.
- tpose_rest.py makes the T-pose each motion-capture clip starts with its skeleton's rest pose.
