# Movement
How the character moves: walking, jumping, dashing, turning, collision, size and moving ground, and what it can do ([Capabilities](#capabilities)). The abilities built on top of these (crouching, wall running, ledges, climbing, ropes and the rest) are in [abilities.md](abilities.md) and [traversal.md](traversal.md).

Most of the settings named here are on the BasicMovementMode asset (`Assets/ScriptableObjects/BasicMovementMode.asset`), under the section named; the rest are on the Character prefab's components (`Assets/Prefabs/Character.prefab`).

## Movement Components
The movement mode is made of components: parts of the movement, each with its own settings, listed under Components at the bottom of the BasicMovementMode asset's inspector. They're Bounce Pads, Ziplines, Rope Swinging, Ledges, Vaulting, Pushing And Pulling, Wall Climbing, Swimming, Wall Running, Wall Sliding, Rolling, Crouching (which slides too, unless its Sliding Enabled is off), Wading, Fall & Air Handling, Walking & Running, Dashing and Jumping. Every part of the movement is a component; the mode itself keeps only what they share (the character's posture, and the Jump and Crouch presses).

Components act at different points of a step: most take the character over (the first in the list that can has it). Fall & Air Handling's gravity acts every step before that, and its fall speed limit last of all, whatever has the character. When nothing has it: Crouching changes how it walks; then Fall & Air Handling's ground friction takes away the momentum it carries and Walking & Running walks it (friction first, since walking makes up the difference to the momentum friction leaves), or in the air Fall & Air Handling steers it; then Dashing and Jumping act, a dash before the jump that cancels it. Those last four keep that order at the end of the list. Turning Jumping off leaves the character without jumps from the ground or in mid-air, but Jump still jumps off walls, ropes and ziplines, and a press counts for Jumping's Jump Input Buffer either way.

- **Turning one off.** Its toggle turns it off, and the movement goes on as if it weren't there. Turned off while it has the character (riding a zipline, say), it lets go.
- **Removing and adding.** The ✕ button removes a component, and Add Component adds one the mode doesn't have yet, at its default settings. A mode has one of each.
- **Order.** Where several components could take over the character in the same step, the one higher in the list does: a bounce pad launches the character out of whatever it's doing on it, so Bounce Pads is first, and a climbable wall is climbed rather than run along or slid down, so Wall Climbing comes before Wall Running and Wall Sliding; deep water takes the character out of a roll, a slide or a dash into swimming, but a ladder or climbable wall rising out of it is climbed, so Swimming comes after Wall Climbing and before the rest. The arrows move a component up and down. Only the order within each point of the step matters.
- **Settings shared between components.** A few components are shaped by another's settings, which they use whether that one is turned on or not (or its defaults, if it's been removed): a vault is over something lower than Ledges' grab heights, a wall slid down reaches up the body by them, and is as upright as Wall Running's Wall Run Max Wall Tilt; anything low (a roll, a ledge climbed onto crouched) is at Crouching's Crouch Height, and every Crouch press counts for its Crouch Input Buffer. Air steering and a roll's steering aim for Walking & Running's speeds, and every ability that works with gravity (a jump's launch speed, a rope's swing, a slide down a wall, a bounce pad's height) uses Fall & Air Handling's Gravity Strength, as a wall run uses its Air Acceleration. Climbing over the top of a climbable surface hands the character to the ledge climb, so it happens only with Ledges turned on.

In code, components are `MovementComponent`s (`Assets/Scripts/MovementModes/Components`), and a mode made of them is a `ComposedMovementMode`: a new kind of movement is a new mode type listing the components it uses. `ComposedMovementMode.Get<T>()`, `Add<T>()` and `Remove<T>()` find, add and remove them at runtime.

### Tuning Presets
A tuning preset (a MovementTuningPreset asset: Create > Scriptable Objects > MovementModes > Tuning Preset) saves movement settings to apply again: a whole tuning you liked, or just a few settings (a floatier jump, wall running turned off). Save Tuning as Preset, at the bottom of a movement mode's inspector, saves all of its settings into a new preset.

In a preset's inspector, each component it covers is listed with a tick beside each setting: ticked settings are set by the preset and the rest left alone (changing a value ticks it; All and None tick or untick a whole component, and Turned On is the component's on/off switch). Add Component's Settings adds another component, unticked. Save From Mode replaces the preset with the Movement Mode's tuning now, and Apply To Mode sets the preset's settings on it, with Undo, and says what changed. The Movement Mode is the Character prefab's unless another is picked.

A preset only sets values and on/off switches: it never adds, removes or reorders a mode's components, and a setting of a component the mode doesn't have is left out (and reported). Presets are made and edited in the editor only. How tuning works as a whole (what changes when you save, what to tune together) is in [Tuning the Character](tuning.md).

## Walking and Sprinting
The character moves at its Walk Speed, or slower with partial stick input, and sprints at its Run Speed while Sprint is held: Left Shift (keyboard) or pressing the left stick (gamepad). Both are in the BasicMovementMode asset's Walking & Running component; a Run Speed at or below the Walk Speed turns sprinting off. Turned off, movement input doesn't move the character on the ground (it still steers in the air, toward these speeds).

Sprinting works in the air too: steering toward the run speed, and keeping a sprint's momentum through a jump without stacking more on top.

Gravity, falling and steering in the air are the Fall & Air Handling component's: its Gravity & Falling section (Gravity Strength, Max Fall Speed, and the Coyote Time Gravity Curve, the gentler gravity just after walking off an edge), its Air Steering section (how much and how soon the character steers in the air, and how steering comes back after a wall jump), and its Landing section (Grounded Dampen Horizontal Inertia, below). Turned off, the character doesn't fall, steer in the air or lose momentum on the ground.

Momentum carries onto the ground: landing from a sprint jump while walking keeps the sprint's speed, which eases down to the walk speed with the ground's friction (Grounded Dampen Horizontal Inertia).

Walking is at its speed at once, except after an ability that hands the character back on the ground slower than it walks (the end of a vault, or of a climb up onto a ledge): it then picks up from the speed it was going to its walking (or running) speed, at the rate Walking & Running's Ability Pick Up Time sets (the seconds from a standstill to the run speed), rather than snapping to it. Walking or sprinting only makes up the difference between the momentum the character carries the way it's steering and its speed: it moves at whichever is faster, never at the two added together.

(They used to add up: just after a landing the character moved at its air speed plus its walking speed, and hopping again at once, or clipping a ledge's lip for a step or two, carried both into the air, building up to several times the walking speed.)

### How the gaits are shown
Quaternius's walk is made for a slow stroll and their jog (what sprinting shows) for a quick run, so the mannequin's own brisk walk and run fill in the speeds between them. Each loop's speed is measured from its strides when the mannequin is generated (`MotionCaptureLocomotion.StrideSpeed`, logged by MannequinGenerator), and the Locomotion blend tree places it there.

The brisk walk is also shown a little past the speed it's made for (MannequinGenerator's `BriskWalkReach` and `BriskWalkPlayback`), so a walking speed a little faster than people naturally walk still looks like a walk; the trade is that its planted feet slide a little there. A Walk Speed beyond that shows the run blending in. Past the sprint's own speed, it plays faster to keep up (see [Walking and running](animation.md#walking-and-running)). Sprinting plays Quaternius's jog at its own pace, with its feet planted (see [Character Model and Animations](animation.md)).

## Jumping
Press Jump (Space, or the south face button) on the ground, or just after walking off an edge (Coyote Time), to jump. A press a moment before landing (within Jump Input Buffer) jumps on landing.

A jump is set by how high it goes. A tap rises the Min Jump Height, and Jump held to the top of the jump rises the Max Jump Height. Letting go in between cuts the rise short, but never below the min height; let go of past it, the jump stops rising at once. The launch speed is worked out from the max height and the character's gravity, for the physics steps, so the heights are exact. Both heights scale with the character's size.

Max Jumps sets how many jumps it gets before landing (1 for none in mid-air); an air jump rises the same heights from where it's made. Bumping a ceiling ends the rise.

All of it is in the BasicMovementMode asset's Jumping component, Coyote Time included; turning it off, or a Max Jump Height of 0, turns jumping off.

## Dash
Press F (keyboard) or right trigger (gamepad) to dash: a short burst in the movement direction relative to the camera, or straight ahead without input. A dash moves in a straight line, ignoring gravity and steering, and hands over to momentum when it ends instead of stopping dead.

It has a cooldown, a short input buffer (pressing just before the cooldown ends still dashes), one dash per button press, and a limited number of air dashes that reset on landing. Jumping during a dash cancels it straight into the jump, keeping part of the dash's speed. Dashing during a jump cancels the jump's upward momentum, and the jump doesn't resume afterwards.

The character plays a dash animation: it slides low in the dash direction with its legs trailing behind.

All of it is tunable in the BasicMovementMode asset's Dashing component, including a speed curve over the dash and how much speed is kept afterwards. Turning it off, or a Dash Duration of 0, turns dashing off.

## Turning and Leaning
The character turns at once where it's steered, so it's always responsive, but its model (on the model parent, separate from the character's root for this) turns after it, naturally. All of this is tuned in the AnimationController's Smooth Rotation Variables and Leaning sections, on the Character prefab.

**Turning.** The model eases into and out of each turn (a damped spring on its yaw, set by Rotation Fix Speed, rather than snapping most of the way round at the start). It turns no faster than Max Turn Speed Standing while standing still, falling to Max Turn Speed Moving at Full Turn Speed At and above, so a sprinting character sweeps round a turn instead of spinning on the spot. It pitches after the character's body (see [Pitch](#collision)) about its feet, as quickly.

**Leaning into turns.** It leans into the curve the character actually runs, by the Turn Lean share of the lean that would balance it: the angle whose tangent is how hard its path turns it (its velocity projected onto the way the model faces, times how fast its path curves) over gravity. So a sharp turn the model is still coming round to leans little.

**Leaning with speed changes.** It leans forward as it speeds up and back as it stops, by the Acceleration Lean share of the balancing lean.

**Limits.** Either way, it leans no further than its actual speed allows (Max Lean Per Speed, in degrees per m/s, up to Max Lean), so walking leans less than sprinting, even round a sharp corner. At a steady speed or standing still it's upright.

As the model turns about its feet, foot placement locks each planted foot where it was put down (see [IK](animation.md#ik)), so the feet don't slide round with it. Facings that abilities set (a dash, roll, slide, wall run, wall slide, wall climb, ledge, or holding an object) are turned to almost at once (Ability Turn Time), without leaning, and spinning ground turns the model with it.

Only the model turns and leans: the character's collision and the camera, which follow the root, don't.

## Collision
MovementController resolves the character's movement against the world itself, with sweeps of the character's body rather than the physics solver. The body (`MovementController.Body`, a `CharacterBody`) is everything collision knows of the character's shape: its size, sweeping it, whether it fits somewhere, and pushing it out of what's moved into it. It also gives the character's proportions, which the abilities measure it by rather than by the collider's dimensions: how tall it stands (`StandingHeight`: grip, hang and reach heights are shares of it) and how far it reaches out in front of its feet, behind them and to either side (`FrontExtent`, `BackExtent`, `SideExtent`: how near it comes to a wall it faces, runs beside or hangs from). It's shaped as one of Unity's primitive colliders (`PrimitiveBody`): a capsule (the Character prefab's, upright on its feet), a box or a sphere, centred on the feet or off them.

**The body isn't a collider.** The movement sweeps and overlaps the body's shape itself, and pushes it out of the world with a private collider of its shape, turned on only while it's used (in a step the body overlaps something), so nothing else meets it (`MovementController.QueryCollider`): the character needs no collider to move, and has none: what meets it (a thrown ball, a trigger it walks into) meets its model's hitboxes (see [Hitboxes](animation.md#hitboxes)). A collider given to the character anyway (the MovementController's Body Collider) is kept in the body's shape (sized, crouched, laid down) as it changes, and changes nothing about how it moves (the golden paths check every run both ways: see [Testing](testing.md)).

**The body as authored.** The MovementController's Body Definition (a `BodyDefinition` asset: the Character prefab's is `Assets/ScriptableObjects/Character Body.asset`) holds the body (a `BodyShape`: its kind, size, where it sits over the feet, and its Lying Thickness), and the body is shaped from it as the character wakes; without one, the body is the Body Collider as it's authored (or, with no collider either, the default body, `BodyShape.Default`: an upright capsule). `MovementController.SetBody` gives the character another body (a model's, say: see [Models](animation.md)), replacing the collider (if it has one) with one of its kind if need be, and like growing it waits for room.

**A body of several parts.** A Body Definition's Extra Parts are more primitives beside its shape, together one body: a long creature's back and head (the fox's: see [Creatures](creatures.md)), where one box round all of it would reach out with its corners. The movement's sweeps, overlaps and push-outs cover every part; the body is as tall as its highest part's top above the feet (its standing height), and reaches as far ahead, behind and to the sides as its furthest part does. Held low, every part is kept under the lowered top: one already under it stays where it is, the others are pushed down under it, made shorter or thinner only where they must be. Lying, only its shape lies down, its extra parts left out. Parts may sit above the feet (a back held over its legs), which only hovering over steps can stand on: standing on its feet (Hover Over Steps off), such a body's feet would sink into the floor until its bottom met it.

**Hovering over steps.** With the MovementController's Hover Over Steps on (as the Character and Fox prefabs have it), the body's bottom is held the step height (its Max Step Height) above the feet, standing or crouched (lying, it rests on its feet): the movement's sweeps and overlaps are of the body shortened from the bottom that far (a box or upright capsule, never made narrower: a capsule no shorter than it's wide; a sphere, or a capsule lying, isn't shortened), its top, its Height and its collider staying as they are. What's no taller than a step passes under it, and the feet are kept on the ground by probes instead: a ray straight down under the feet (or, where it falls between two treads or boxes, the highest of a few round them), and, where none meets anything, the ground check's sphere resting on the edge of something beside the feet (feet just past an edge). Each step the feet go onto the ground they find, from a step's height above them (up onto a step, as their middle passes over its edge, not as the body reaches it) to a step's height below (down a step or a slope, even standing still, so they don't perch on an edge over the step below), as far as the body has room to move there; landing, they stop on it; rising or held in the air (hanging, climbing, swimming), they're never left inside it (walking up a beach). So the body never rests on an edge or a corner, and the character's root stays on the ground under it. What's taller than a step meets the body: a wall stops it, and the edge of a ramp rising into its bottom is slid up. A long body over stairs meets their next step while its feet are still below it: it steps up onto it if it's no more than a step above the ground in front of it (each step of a flight is), and rides up the flight on the steps ahead of its feet, which hang above the treads under them; a wall taller than a step, with the floor in front of it, is never stepped onto. Resting on something counts as being on the ground if there's ground under its feet within a step and the lift (a long body's back end on the steps behind, going down a flight: any of its bottom's contacts, an edge as much as a tread); a body that meets something with no ground under its feet tips off it (perched on the edge of a drop), or slides down it (too steep to stand on). Off, the body stands on the feet, and steps up onto what it meets (below).

**Postures.** The body is held standing, low or lying (a `BodyPosture`), the feet staying where they are. Each step the first movement component in list order to hold it (its `Posture`) has its way: low at Crouching's Crouch Height while crouched, sliding or rolling (the shape lowered from the top), lying while swimming underwater (the shape laid down along the way the character faces, resting on the feet: an upright capsule lies along it, a box's height and depth swap, no thicker than the body's Lying Thickness, when it has one: a swimmer is thinner front to back than it's wide). However it's held, the character's origin is the bottom of its body, what it stands (or lies) on. Lowering always has room; any other change waits for it, held low meanwhile, and with nothing holding it the body stands again once there's room.

**Turning.** A body that isn't round about its feet (lying, a box, a shape off to one side) turns into somewhere else as the character turns, so it turns only as far as it has room to: the body is checked for room facing the new way, and the turn is shortened until it fits (a turn isn't swept).

**Pitch.** The body can also pitch, nose up or down about its feet (`MovementController.BodyPitch`): swimming underwater, it tips along the way it's steered to swim, up to Swimming's Swim Max Pitch. Only the body pitches: the character itself (its Rigidbody and transform) only ever turns about the vertical, so its facing, the ground probe under its feet and gravity are unchanged, and the model is tilted to match (see [Turning and Leaning](#turning-and-leaning)). A component holds a pitch each step (`MovementData.targetPitch`), and without one the body levels out. Like a turn, a pitch is made only as far as the body has room for, and any body turns with the character while it's pitched. The physics scene's own collider on the character isn't pitched (only the body's queries are), so a trigger the character passes through sees it level. Steering free of the ground (`FreeSteering`: the movement input the way the camera looks, Jump to rise and Crouch to sink, facing the way it heads across and pitching along the way it heads) is shared, for underwater swimming now and moving through the air later. It doesn't pitch down into a floor it's blocked by (or up into a ceiling).

Each physics step it:
- pushes the character out of anything that moved into them since the last step, so moving walls and platforms push the character instead of passing through
- slides along walls and walkable slopes (hovering over steps, only a slope rising into the body's bottom: the top edge of something taller than a step is a wall)
- puts the feet on the ground: hovering over steps, where the probes find it (see above); standing on its feet, by stepping the body up stairs and low ledges, anything up to the step height, at any pace (carried far enough onto the step that it doesn't perch on the edge and slide back off), and snapping it down slopes and stairs. A step up, or a drop down onto a step, happens in a physics step (in the air too: the feet up onto a ledge's top at a jump's height); the movement reports how far (`MovementData.stepChange`), and the model is eased over it (see [Stepping up](abilities.md))
- slides the character off surfaces too steep to stand on. Standing on its feet, that includes ledge edges caught by its rounded bottom; but the edge of a step (one with walkable ground no further than the step height below the feet) is ground, so on stairs whose treads are shallower than the body is wide, as the levels' are, it walks and sprints down resting on the steps' edges instead of floating off the top step, even where the ground check's sphere (a little narrower than the body, Ground Check Radius on the MovementController, or 90% of the body's footprint at 0) doesn't reach them

Momentum that a collision blocks is lost: bumping a ceiling ends the jump, and momentum into a wall doesn't build up and fling the character once they move clear of it. Each step's MovementData records which sides were blocked (`collisions`, as Unity's CollisionFlags), and the next step can read them (`previousCollisions`).

## Character Size
The character can be resized at runtime with `ACCharacterController.SetScale(Vector3)`, or by editing the Scale field in the ACCharacterController's inspector while playing. x is width, y height and z depth, relative to the character as authored; any mix of positive values works, including non-uniform and fractional scales.

Negative, zero and non-finite scales are refused with an error (the inspector field reverts), and values below `MinimumScale` (in `ACCharacterController.cs`) are raised to it. Only the model parent is scaled, never the character's own transform; a scaled root (by itself or a parent) is reported as an error, since it would break collision.

Everything else follows:
- **Collision:** the body's height scales with y and a capsule's radius with the larger of x and z (capped at half the height, so a wide, flat character doesn't get a body taller than itself). Step height, ground snapping, the grounded tolerance, the ground check and the skin width (the small gap kept from surfaces) scale with it, so a tiny character obeys the same collision rules as a full-size one.
- **Room to grow:** a change of size takes a moment (Scale Change Duration, on the ACCharacterController). Growing pauses while there isn't room, under a low ceiling say, and carries on once there is; shrinking always happens.
- **Movement:** by default it scales in proportion: speeds, accelerations and gravity scale with height, and timings stay the same, so the character moves exactly like a scaled copy of itself. It crosses its own height in the same time, and a jump lasts as long and goes as high for its size, so a small character doesn't look zippy and a big one doesn't look floaty.

  A test (`ScaleTests.AtAnySize_TheCharacterMovesLikeAScaledCopyOfItself`, at the sizes in its `AnySize` list) runs it over a course of ramps, a step, a jump and a dash, from far smaller to far bigger than authored, and checks every physics step lands it in the same place for its size as at full size.

  The MovementController's Size Scaling setting can instead scale it naturally, as real animals do (speeds with the square root of height, gravity unchanged, so smaller characters look quicker for their size), or keep speeds unchanged.
- **Mass** scales with volume.
- **Animation:** the Animator gets speeds divided by the model's stride scale, so planted feet stay put at any size or stretch. Vertical speed is divided by how speeds scale, so the rising and falling poses fall at the same points of a jump. Foot placement's step limits scale too, and IK places hands and feet right when the model is stretched on one axis.

  Unity's humanoid IK gets stretched models wrong in two ways, and CharacterIK corrects both. It reports foot goals as if the model were scaled evenly by its width, so a taller-stretched model's feet were placed below the floor, and a shorter one's above it. And it solves each limb from where the limb starts (the hip joint, or the collarbone), moving the hand or foot further from there across and along the model than asked, by the model's width and depth scale over its height scale: a foot was put well past a goal just in front of the hip on a deeper model, and a hand on a wider model missed by a wide margin.

  Hands and feet now land on their goals on every model and stretch tested (`AnimationTests.MovingAGoal_MovesTheHandOrFootAsFar_AtAnyStretch`, which holds the tolerances).
- **Feet on the ground:** every model's soles stay on the floor at any size, including stretched on one axis only. Prepare Character Models measures how far each model's soles stand from its origin and stores it as its source's Ground Offset, and straightens rotated nodes above the skeleton, which used to distort stretched models (see [Character models](animation.md#character-models)).
- **The model's measurements**, the eye point and the placeholder all follow, and models loaded while the character is resized are measured at its size.
- **The camera:** first person follows the eye point. Third person's pivot height, shoulder offset and distance, and the head bob, scale with the camera rig's Target Size Reference, which a spawned character hands the camera (its model parent), through its spawner's camera event (see [Spawning the Character](spawning.md)).

  Speed effects (the speed-based field of view and head bob strength) judge the target's speed relative to its size, dividing it by its height, so a character at its own top speed gets the same effect at any size.

  Every other distance and speed the camera uses scales with the target too: follow lag, landing dip and its speed threshold, collision probe and minimum distance, hide distance, shake, teleport distance and the clip planes (near always, far only for a bigger target, so a small one still sees the world). See [Camera System](camera.md).

## Moving Ground
The character rides along with whatever it stands on. Each physics step, before moving, the character is carried by however far its ground moved, and turned with it if it spins. It then moves relative to the ground as normal. Leaving moving ground, by jumping or walking off, keeps the ground's velocity, so the character doesn't stop dead in mid-air.

These options can be toggled in the MovementController's Moving Ground section (Move With Ground, Rotate With Ground, Inherit Ground Velocity). Ground that moves further than Max Ground Carry Per Step in one physics step is treated as teleporting and doesn't carry the character. Moving the character somewhere at once (a respawn, a checkpoint) goes through `MovementController.Teleport`, which forgets the ground it stood on; set the Rigidbody's position directly instead and, standing, the next step carries the character back to where it stood on its ground.

Moving platforms should be kinematic Rigidbodies moved in FixedUpdate with interpolation enabled; the MovingPlatform component does this. Tools > Character Showcase > Create Foot Placement Test Area adds a sliding platform, an elevator and a spinning platform to try it.

## Capabilities
`MovementController.Capabilities` says what the character can do, as it's tuned and sized now, read-only: how high it steps up and how steep a slope it walks; how tall it stands and crouches; how high its jumps rise; how far each kind of jump crosses (a jump, a sprint jump, a sprint jump and an air dash, and double jumps when it has air jumps); how high each kind of climb reaches (stepping up, jumping up onto something, jumping to a ledge and climbing it); and how far a slide, a roll and a wall run carry it, and how far a wall run falls. Each is in metres at the character's size, or NaN where it can't do that at all (an ability turned off).

The values that are exact by construction come straight from the settings: the step height (MovementController's Max Step Height), the walkable slope (Max Slope Angle), the jump heights (Min Jump Height and Max Jump Height), the stand height (the collider) and the crouch height (Crouch Height's share of it). The rest are measured by running the character's own movement: a copy of the character (its settings, movement mode, collider and size, none of its state) is stepped through each move in a world of its own, far below everything else, faster than real time and without rendering (`MovementSimulation`). The movement is deterministic (a fixed physics step, its own clock, `StepTime`, and no randomness), so this gives exact answers that can't drift from what the movement does. Moves with a choice of timing (when to dash, when to jump again) are measured at their best timing, found by trying each. Measuring takes a moment, so the capabilities are measured the first time they're asked for and again after the movement mode's settings, the character's size or its collider change. A measurement is shared by every character tuned, sized and shaped the same (another character, or one put back as it was), so it's only made once.

The levelspec tool's movement checks hold levels to the default character's capabilities: Tools/LevelSpec/envelope.json is written from them (Tools > Character Showcase > Level > Specs > Write Movement Envelope; see [Level specs](level-specs.md)), written again whenever the tuning is saved in the editor, with a Console log of what changed; a test fails if it's out of date anyway.
