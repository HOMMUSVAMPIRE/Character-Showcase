# Creatures

Characters don't have to be humanoids. The first creature is a fox: a wolf-sized, low-poly fox with flat-coloured regions (an orange coat, a white chest, muzzle and tail tip, dark legs, ear tips and nose), on four legs, with its own body, movement and animations. It's built the way the mannequin is: generated in Blender by scripts, then set up in Unity by a generator.

## Playing the fox

`Assets/Prefabs/Fox.prefab` is a character like `Character.prefab` (it's a variant of it), with the fox's body, movement mode and model. To play it in a scene, set the scene's character spawner's Character Prefab to it (see [Spawning the character](spawning.md)); the camera follows it as it does the humanoid, from its eyes.

## What the fox is

- **Its body** (`Assets/Models/Creatures/Fox/Fox Body.asset`, a Body Definition) is rounded, with no corners to catch on things: its back is two capsules lying along it, one above the other (it's narrower than it's deep), from its rump to its chest, and its head a sphere ahead of them, as high as its head is (not its ears or its tail): a shape of several parts (see [Collision](movement.md#collision)). It's held above its legs: the lower capsule's bottom is a step above its paws, which only hovering over steps stands on (so the fox keeps Hover Over Steps on), its paws kept on the ground by probes. It's a shape the movement moves, not a collider: what meets the fox meets its model's hitboxes, on its body, head, tail and legs (see [Hitboxes](animation.md#hitboxes)). Up and over a ramp and up a flight of stairs its paws stay on the ground; down a flight as steep as the levels', its back end, well behind its paws, rests on the steps behind, its paws a little above the treads under them (the model's paw placement reaches them down). Being longer than it's wide, it turns only as far as it has room to (see [Collision](movement.md#collision)).
- **Its movement** is `Assets/ScriptableObjects/Fox Movement.asset`: the humanoid's movement mode with what a fox can't do turned off (ledges, vaulting, climbing, ladders, pushing and pulling, ropes, ziplines, wall slides, rolling and dashing) and its own tuning: it trots at its Walk Speed and gallops sprinting (Run Speed), jumps once, crouches (stalks) without sliding, swims and wall-runs (see below). Tune it there.
- **Its model** is `Assets/Models/Creatures/Fox/Fox.fbx`, a Generic rig (not a Unity Humanoid): Hips, Spine1 and Spine2, Neck, Head, Muzzle, the ears, four tail bones, and each leg's bones (FrontUpperLeg, FrontLowerLeg and FrontPaw; HindUpperLeg, HindLowerLeg, HindFoot and HindPaw; each `_L` and `_R`). Its model prefab carries a transform named Eyes on its head, which the character's eye point follows (a model that isn't a humanoid marks its eyes so: see `CharacterModelHost.EyesName`).
- **Its animations** are in place and loop: Idle, Walk, Trot, Gallop, Jump Rise, Jump Fall, Crouch Idle, Crouch Walk, Turn Step (stepping in place, for turning on the spot), Swim and Swim Idle (a dog paddle, and treading water). Walking, trotting and stalking, its tail sways gently side to side once a stride, rippling down it, counterbalancing its legs; galloping, it runs flat out, its neck lowered and stretched forward, its ears folded back, its tail streaming out behind. Its Animator Controller (`Fox.controller`) plays Locomotion (Idle, or Turn Step as it turns standing, by TurnSpeed; then Walk, Trot and Gallop by Speed), Crouch (Crouch Idle and Crouch Walk by Speed, while Crouching), Swim (Swim Idle and Swim by Speed, while Swimming), Wall Run (Locomotion's gaits by Speed, while WallRunning) and Airborne (Jump Fall and Jump Rise by VerticalSpeed, while not Grounded, Swimming or WallRunning), driven by its own Animator parameter translator, as the humanoid's is.

## Swimming

The fox swims with the humanoid's Swimming, tuned on its movement mode: its feet hang Swim Depth (a share of its height) below the surface, so its back is at the waterline and its raised head out of the water; it paddles at its Swim Speed (faster sprinting, Swim Sprint Speed), and jumps out (Swim Jump Height) onto edges low enough. It doesn't dive (Swim Dive Speed is 0): a fox swims at the surface, and a box body laid down to swim underwater would stand on end. The Swim and Swim Idle clips draw it at its standing height above its feet, as at the swim depth that puts it at the waterline; its paws aren't placed while it swims.

## Wall running

The fox wall-runs with the humanoid's WallRunning, tuned on its movement mode: its middle is held Wall Run Clearance out from the wall, a little beyond its side, and its runs start from lower jumps (Wall Run Min Height). Run at a wall at an angle and jump onto it, steering along it or on at it (as steeply as Wall Run Max Steer Angle lets a run start, moving along the wall at least Wall Run Min Speed): its box meets the wall with a corner, often while its side is still well off the wall, and it turns along the wall and is drawn in to it (see [Wall-running](abilities.md)). PawPlacementIK rolls its body toward the wall (about its legs' tops, along the way it runs) until its legs reach the wall (held near the wall, a little: about a third of a right angle), as far as Max Wall Roll, easing in and out over Wall Roll Blend Time, and puts each paw on the wall's face: one the gait would take through the wall goes onto it, and a planted one is drawn onto it, held out by its leg's Wall Clearance (how far its paw's mesh reaches beyond its contact). It plays its gaits as it runs (the Wall Run state).


## Paw placement

Humanoid IK (foot placement, hands on ledges) works through Unity's humanoid rig, so the fox has its own: PawPlacementIK, on its model prefab, plants its paws on slopes, stairs and bumps. The animations are authored for flat ground at its feet; after the Animator poses it each frame, PawPlacementIK:

- **lays its body along the ground.** It probes the ground at points along the body (Body Probe Reach ahead of and behind its middle) and fits a line through them, then rests the line on the highest of them, so on stairs it runs along the steps' edges. The body is pitched to the line's slope (as far as Max Body Pitch) about its own middle, the point between its legs' tops, and raised or lowered so its shoulders and hips stand as high above the line as the animation has them stand above the floor. Up or down a ramp or a flight of stairs, the fox leans with it, along it, rather than a step at a time. Its tail and neck are turned back against the pitch (Counter Pitch Bones): the head looks ahead rather than at the sky or its paws, and climbing, the tail is held up off the slope behind; descending, it follows the body, up off the steps behind, and its tip is kept clear of the steps rising behind it (the Tail1 bone's Ground Clearance: turned up as far as that takes);
- **lowers an end of its body** as far as a planted paw there must reach further down than the animation reaches (a hind paw on a low tread drops the hips, not the shoulders), no further than Max Body Drop, and never so far that its shoulders or hips come nearer the ground under them than Leg Top Clearance;
- **bends each leg** (a two-bone solve of its upper and lower bones) to put its paw on the ground under it. That's the highest ground under its toes, under its joint (the wrist, or the hind leg's hock), and under the leg's middle joint where that leans out ahead of the paw (a hind leg's knee over a landing's edge as its paw leaves the last step). Each leg's middle joint always bends the way it folds (its Bend Direction): a front leg's elbow back, a hind leg's stifle forward. It keeps the animation's lift, so swinging paws clear the ground, and the paw's animated turn, tilted to the slope while planted. A swinging paw looks a little ahead of its toes (Swing Look Ahead) for a step up, higher ground than its slope carries on to, and is lifted onto it, so up a flight of stairs it comes up over the next step's edge rather than through its riser;
- **locks planted paws** (Paw Lock): as the animation puts a paw down, where it was put down is remembered (relative to the ground under it, so it rides moving ground), and the paw is held there until the animation lifts it, or the animation's paw gets further from it than Max Lock Distance; the lock blends in and out over Lock Blend Time. The model turns after the character, about its feet, which would otherwise drag planted paws round with it, a long body's furthest of all, and any mismatch between a stride and the fox's speed slides them. A held paw stays on the ground where it was put down, at the animation's lift above it, so on a slope it doesn't float or sink as the animation's paw moves on.

- **puts each paw on a foothold**: as a paw swings, where it'll be put down is chosen among where the animation has it and Foothold Reach ahead or behind, whichever has its ground nearest the body's line (so its leg is nearest the length the animation gives it), and not where its joints or toes would be in the ground there (a hind paw by the riser behind it, going down a flight, would have its hock in the step above; a paw at the back of a tread, going up, its toes in the riser ahead). On even ground every foothold is as good, and the paw goes where the animation has it. Each paw also moves with its leg's top as the body pitches, so the legs keep the upright stance the animation gives them;
- **shortens and quickens its strides on a slope** (Stride Shortening, down to Shortest Stride): a planted paw stays put while the body goes on over it the length of a stance, rising or falling as far along the slope, more than a leg can stretch or fold to. Its paws are drawn in toward its legs' tops, and the Animator played faster by as much while it moves (so the paws keep pace with the ground);
- **bends its body into a turn** (Turn Bend Bones: the spine, neck and head toward it, the tail away, so its tip swings round too), by how fast the model turns, as far as Max Turn Bend at Full Bend Turn Speed. Turning standing, its Animator blends from Idle into Turn Step (stepping in place) by its TurnSpeed, and its paws, lifted and locked again where the turning body puts them down, step round.

The ground the paws stand on is found where it's drawn: a moving platform's Rigidbody is interpolated, drawn between its physics steps as the fox is, while a ray hits it at its latest step, so each hit on one is moved to where it's drawn, and each paw's eased ground is carried along with what it stands on. Riding a platform up and down, the paws and body stay steady on it.

A paw counts as planted while the animation has it low (within Planted Height of the lowest it's had it lately: a hind paw rolls a little onto its toes through its stance) and isn't carrying it forward, relative to the body, faster than Swing Speed: a planted paw goes back as the body passes over it, a swinging one forward, however low a hind paw swings with its toes curled down.

The ground is probed from inside the character's body (Probe Height above its feet), where nothing else can be, so a probe never starts inside a step. The body's line is found each frame, in the world, so it climbs a flight as smoothly as the flight rises, though the character's feet jump a step at a time; only how far its ends are lowered is eased (Body Smooth Time), as is each paw's ground, in the world (Paw Smooth Time), never into it. Placement fades out in the air (over Grounded Fade Time), so jumps play as animated.

The fox's long, flat-bottomed body rests on the step under its front end going up a flight and its back end going down, so its middle is well above the flight and the paws at its other end well below its feet: the ground is looked for down to Max Step Down below them.

FoxGenerator adds PawPlacementIK when it builds the model prefab. It finds the legs by their bones' names, puts each paw's contact at its toes (which stay down through a stride while the paw rolls over them), and sets the legs' bend directions and the tail and neck to turn back against the pitch. Its other settings are PawPlacementIK's defaults, since the prefab is built anew each time: tune them on the component's defaults, or on the prefab after generating. A model stepped by hand (the tests do) calls its `Solve` after each `Animator.Update`; in the game it runs in LateUpdate while the Animator plays.

## Generating the fox

Tools > Character Showcase > Generate Fox (or `-executeMethod CharacterShowcase.EditorTools.FoxGenerator.GenerateFromCommandLine`, with `-skipBlender` to rebuild from the Fox.fbx that's there) runs `Tools/Fox/generate_fox.py` in Blender, which builds, rigs, skins and animates the fox (its clips in `Tools/Fox/fox_animations.py`), then imports it and builds its controller, model prefab, translator, model source and Fox.prefab. Its body and movement mode are made only if they aren't there, and left as they're tuned. Run the script by hand with `--preview <folder>` to render it from the side, front and three-quarters, and from the side through each clip.

A fox's legs fold as a dog's do: a front leg's elbow points back (lifting, the forearm flexes forward and the wrist folds the paw back under it), a hind leg's stifle forward. Each leg flexes only at its middle joint (elbow or stifle) to keep its paw from going below the ground, and always the way it folds, keeping the line from its shoulder or hip to its paw. A joint bent one way on one key and the other on the next snaps across between them, sweeping the paw through the floor for a frame. The clips are keyed at 120 per second, so a galloping leg landing straight and flexing fast stays off the floor between keys too.

The gaits are authored for speeds (`SPEEDS` in fox_animations.py) that FoxGenerator's Speed blend thresholds match, so the paws keep to the ground at them; change both together.
