# Creatures

Characters don't have to be humanoids. The first creature is a fox: a wolf-sized, low-poly fox with flat-coloured regions (an orange coat, a white chest, muzzle and tail tip, dark legs, ear tips and nose), on four legs, with its own body, movement and animations. It's built the way the mannequin is: generated in Blender by scripts, then set up in Unity by a generator.

## Playing the fox

`Assets/Prefabs/Fox.prefab` is a character like `Character.prefab` (it's a variant of it), with the fox's body, movement mode and model. To play it in a scene, set the scene's character spawner's Character Prefab to it (see [Spawning the character](spawning.md)); the camera follows it as it does the humanoid, from its eyes.

## What the fox is

- **Its body** is a box (`Assets/Models/Creatures/Fox/Fox Body.asset`, a Body Definition): about its body's size, from just behind its nose to its rump, standing on its paws, its middle a little ahead of its feet. Not its ears or its tail. Being longer than it's wide, it turns only as far as it has room to (see [Collision](movement.md#collision)), and being flat-bottomed, it's on a step once it's over its top, however far ahead of its feet that is.
- **Its movement** is `Assets/ScriptableObjects/Fox Movement.asset`: the humanoid's movement mode with what a fox can't do turned off (ledges, vaulting, climbing, ladders, pushing and pulling, ropes, ziplines, wall runs and slides, rolling, dashing and, for now, swimming) and its own tuning: it trots at its Walk Speed and gallops sprinting (Run Speed), jumps once, and crouches (stalks) without sliding. Tune it there.
- **Its model** is `Assets/Models/Creatures/Fox/Fox.fbx`, a Generic rig (not a Unity Humanoid): Hips, Spine1 and Spine2, Neck, Head, Muzzle, the ears, four tail bones, and each leg's bones (FrontUpperLeg, FrontLowerLeg and FrontPaw; HindUpperLeg, HindLowerLeg, HindFoot and HindPaw; each `_L` and `_R`). Its model prefab carries a transform named Eyes on its head, which the character's eye point follows (a model that isn't a humanoid marks its eyes so: see `CharacterModelHost.EyesName`).
- **Its animations** are in place and loop: Idle, Walk, Trot, Gallop, Jump Rise, Jump Fall, Crouch Idle and Crouch Walk. Its Animator Controller (`Fox.controller`) plays Locomotion (Idle, Walk, Trot and Gallop by Speed), Crouch (Crouch Idle and Crouch Walk by Speed, while Crouching) and Airborne (Jump Fall and Jump Rise by VerticalSpeed, while not Grounded), driven by its own Animator parameter translator, as the humanoid's is.

## Paw placement

Humanoid IK (foot placement, hands on ledges) works through Unity's humanoid rig, so the fox has its own: PawPlacementIK, on its model prefab, plants its paws on slopes, stairs and bumps. The animations are authored for flat ground at its feet; after the Animator poses it each frame, PawPlacementIK:

- **lays its body along the ground.** It probes the ground at points along the body (Body Probe Reach ahead of and behind its middle) and fits a line through them, then rests the line on the highest of them, so on stairs it runs along the steps' edges. The body is pitched to the line's slope (as far as Max Body Pitch) about its own middle, the point between its legs' tops, and raised or lowered so its shoulders and hips stand as high above the line as the animation has them stand above the floor. Up or down a ramp or a flight of stairs, the fox leans with it, along it, rather than a step at a time. Its tail and neck are turned back against the pitch (Counter Pitch Bones): the head looks ahead rather than at the sky or its paws, and climbing, the tail is held up off the slope behind; descending, it follows the body, up off the steps behind;
- **lowers an end of its body** as far as a planted paw there must reach further down than the animation reaches (a hind paw on a low tread drops the hips, not the shoulders), no further than Max Body Drop, and never so far that its shoulders or hips come nearer the ground under them than Leg Top Clearance;
- **bends each leg** (a two-bone solve of its upper and lower bones) to put its paw on the ground under it. That's the highest ground under its toes, under its joint (the wrist, or the hind leg's hock), and under the leg's middle joint where that leans out ahead of the paw (a hind leg's knee over a landing's edge as its paw leaves the last step). Each leg's middle joint always bends the way it folds (its Bend Direction): a front leg's elbow back, a hind leg's stifle forward. It keeps the animation's lift, so swinging paws clear the ground, and the paw's animated turn, tilted to the slope while planted.

The ground is probed from inside the character's body (Probe Height above its feet), where nothing else can be, so a probe never starts inside a step. The body's line is found each frame, in the world, so it climbs a flight as smoothly as the flight rises, though the character's feet jump a step at a time; only how far its ends are lowered is eased (Body Smooth Time), as is each paw's ground, in the world (Paw Smooth Time), never into it. Placement fades out in the air (over Grounded Fade Time), so jumps play as animated.

The fox's long, flat-bottomed body rests on the step under its front end going up a flight and its back end going down, so its middle is well above the flight and the paws at its other end well below its feet: the ground is looked for down to Max Step Down below them.

FoxGenerator adds PawPlacementIK when it builds the model prefab. It finds the legs by their bones' names, puts each paw's contact at its toes (which stay down through a stride while the paw rolls over them), and sets the legs' bend directions and the tail and neck to turn back against the pitch. Its other settings are PawPlacementIK's defaults, since the prefab is built anew each time: tune them on the component's defaults, or on the prefab after generating. A model stepped by hand (the tests do) calls its `Solve` after each `Animator.Update`; in the game it runs in LateUpdate while the Animator plays.

## Generating the fox

Tools > Character Showcase > Generate Fox (or `-executeMethod CharacterShowcase.EditorTools.FoxGenerator.GenerateFromCommandLine`, with `-skipBlender` to rebuild from the Fox.fbx that's there) runs `Tools/Fox/generate_fox.py` in Blender, which builds, rigs, skins and animates the fox (its clips in `Tools/Fox/fox_animations.py`), then imports it and builds its controller, model prefab, translator, model source and Fox.prefab. Its body and movement mode are made only if they aren't there, and left as they're tuned. Run the script by hand with `--preview <folder>` to render it from the side, front and three-quarters, and from the side through each clip.

A fox's legs fold as a dog's do: a front leg's elbow points back (lifting, the forearm flexes forward and the wrist folds the paw back under it), a hind leg's stifle forward. Each leg flexes only at its middle joint (elbow or stifle) to keep its paw from going below the ground, and always the way it folds, keeping the line from its shoulder or hip to its paw. A joint bent one way on one key and the other on the next snaps across between them, sweeping the paw through the floor for a frame. The clips are keyed at 120 per second, so a galloping leg landing straight and flexing fast stays off the floor between keys too.

The gaits are authored for speeds (`SPEEDS` in fox_animations.py) that FoxGenerator's Speed blend thresholds match, so the paws keep to the ground at them; change both together.
